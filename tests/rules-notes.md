# Rules answer key: likely legal pitfalls

1. **Benefit bands (convention conflicts with law).** Under the 2026 EDD table (DE 2588), 90% applies only up to a $16,163.33 high quarter. A flat band of about $1,120 to $1,129 runs to $20,967.14, then 70% applies up to the $1,765 cap. The product's 19,936.80 cutoff is the 2025 flat-band ceiling. It overpays the $16.2k to $19.9k band (up to $1,380) and leaves a $307 cliff at one cent over. Below $300 the claimant is ineligible, and the $50 minimum doesn't apply.
2. **12 months means calendar months, not 365 days.** The two disagree across leap years (`elig-leap-year`). Service doesn't have to be consecutive.
3. **The 75-mile rule tests employee eligibility, not employer coverage.** An employer with 50+ employees is still FMLA-covered.
4. **FMLA runs with PDL. CFRA doesn't.** Bonding after PDL is usually protected by CFRA alone. PDL is 4 months per pregnancy and is prorated for part-time employees.
5. **FMLA excludes in-laws, grandparents, grandchildren, siblings, registered domestic partners and designated persons.** PFL covers a designated person only from July 1, 2028. An employer may limit CFRA to one designated person per 12 months.
6. **Sick cap is 40 hours or 5 days, whichever is more.** A 40-hour cap undercounts for 10-hour shifts. Use begins on day 90, counting the hire date as day 1. Local ordinances may be more generous.
7. **Bereavement and repro days can be non-consecutive.** They must be used within 3 months. Bereavement excludes a designated person.
8. **"cert" is the employee's minimum 15 calendar days to return certification, not an employer deadline.** The SDI waiting period is 7 calendar days. PFL has no waiting period.
